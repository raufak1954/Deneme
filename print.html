Column Stability & Equivalent Inertia Summary Report

1. Overview of the Eigenvalue Formulation

In structural stability analysis, the exact differential equation for a column under axial load \(P\) with general end restraints yields a boundary value problem. For a uniform column, the displacement function is expressed as:

$$v(z) = A + Bz + C \sin(kz) + D \cos(kz) \quad \text{where } k = \sqrt{\frac{P}{EI}}$$

Applying boundary conditions with rotational springs (\(\alpha_T, \alpha_B\)) and translational springs (\(\beta_T, \beta_B\)) leads to the \(4 \times 4\) transcendental system determinant:

$$\begin{vmatrix} T_T & (kL)^2 & 0 & T_T \\ 0 & R_T & R_T kL & (kL)^2 \\ T_B & [T_B - (kL)^2] & T_B \sin(kL) & T_B \cos(kL) \\ 0 & R_B & [R_B kL \cos(kL) - (kL)^2 \sin(kL)] & [-R_B kL \sin(kL) - (kL)^2 \cos(kL)] \end{vmatrix} = 0$$

Where the dimensionless parameters are defined as:

$$R_T = \frac{\alpha_T L}{EI}, \quad R_B = \frac{\alpha_B L}{EI}, \quad T_T = \frac{\beta_T L^3}{EI}, \quad T_B = \frac{\beta_B L^3}{EI}$$

2. Integration of Equivalent Inertia (\(I_{\text{eff}}\)) for Variable Section Columns

For columns with non-uniform or variable cross-sections (e.g., tapered, haunched, or stepped columns), \(I(z)\) varies along the length \(L\). To avoid complex numerical discretization or variable-coefficient differential equations, an Equivalent Inertia (\(I_{\text{eff}}\)) approach is integrated directly into the determinant matrix model.

Step 1: Compute Equivalent Inertia (\(I_{\text{eff}}\))

Determine \(I_{\text{eff}}\) based on the cross-section variation profile:

  • Linearly Tapered I-Sections (AISC Approach): $$I_{\text{eff}} = C_x \cdot I_{\text{min}}$$ where \(C_x = 1.0 + 0.08\gamma + 0.8\gamma^2\) and \(\gamma = \frac{d_{\text{max}}}{d_{\text{min}}} - 1\).
  • Stepped Columns: $$I_{\text{eff}} \approx I_1 \left(\frac{a}{L}\right) + I_2 \left(\frac{b}{L}\right)$$

Step 2: Update Dimensionless Ratios

Substitute \(I_{\text{eff}}\) into all boundary stiffness ratios:

$$R_T = \frac{\alpha_T L}{E I_{\text{eff}}}, \quad R_B = \frac{\alpha_B L}{E I_{\text{eff}}}$$ $$T_T = \frac{\beta_T L^3}{E I_{\text{eff}}}, \quad T_B = \frac{\beta_B L^3}{E I_{\text{eff}}}$$

Step 3: Solve for Eigenvalue \(kL\)

Find the non-trivial root \(kL\) that satisfies \(\det[M(kL)] = 0\). The physical parameter \(k\) is defined in terms of \(I_{\text{eff}}\):

$$k = \sqrt{\frac{P}{E I_{\text{eff}}}}$$

Step 4: Calculate Critical Buckling Load (\(P_{\text{cr}}\)) & Effective Length (\(K\))

Once \(kL\) is obtained, calculate the critical buckling load:

$$P_{\text{cr}} = \frac{(kL)^2 E I_{\text{eff}}}{L^2}$$

The effective length factor \(K\) relates directly to the eigenvalue \(kL\) by:

$$K = \frac{\pi}{kL}$$
